Decision analysis — Cupertino house at inheritance

Four options for the Cupertino house after inheritance (2045, moderate 3.5% growth scenario shown; all scenarios on the Projections page).

Option 1 — Sell at inheritance and reinvest RECOMMENDED

  • Proceeds at market value ≈ $5,959,754; closing costs ~6% → net $5,602,169
  • $0 capital gains tax (stepped-up basis = FMV at date of death)
  • Reinvested at 6% nominal → ~$299,044/yr after-tax income (dividend drag at ~33% combined)
  • Eliminates: property tax ($72,247/yr), insurance, maintenance, utilities, vacancy/management risk
  • Liquidity: funds available for other opportunities, education, emergencies

Option 2 — Keep as a rental

  • Property tax resets to full market value: $72,247/yr
  • Gross rent ~$60,000/yr (3-bed median ~$5,005/mo — third-party estimate, flag)
  • Operating expenses ≈ $113,787/yr (tax, insurance, maintenance, utilities, management, vacancy)
  • Net operating income ≈ −$53,787/yr → a net loss every year, taxed as ordinary income

Option 3 — Live in it

  • Property tax still resets to $72,247/yr (no Prop 19 exclusion — it was never the parents' principal residence)
  • Plus insurance, utilities, maintenance ≈ $33,740/yr → total housing cost ≈ $105,987/yr
  • Value: you own the asset (appreciates) and live in Cupertino (schools/location) — but the tax reset makes it an expensive place to live

Option 4 — Sell both houses, rent elsewhere, invest everything

  • Both houses sold at stepped-up basis → $0 capital gains on both
  • Eliminates ~$96,256/yr of combined property tax
  • Loses SF rental income ($7,150/mo = $85,800/yr user-provided, flag) and the SF house's housing value

Recommendation

Sell the Cupertino house at inheritance; keep the San Francisco house

1. Sell Cupertino immediately after inheritance → $0 capital gains (stepped-up basis) → reinvest ~$5,602,169 at 6% → ~$299,044/yr after tax with no property-tax drag.

2. Keep the SF house — either live in unit 3 (claim the Prop 19 capped exclusion: new taxable value ≈ $1,938,738, tax ≈ $24,009/yr) or rent all 3 units (rent $85,800/yr comfortably covers a full reassessment of $41,990/yr).

3. Why not keep Cupertino? The Prop 19 reassessment alone (~$72,247/yr by 2045) exceeds plausible net rental income; keeping it destroys wealth versus reinvesting. Living in it costs ~$79K/yr — the worst pure-financial option.

4. Before 2040: fund the trust, decide which house is the parents' principal residence, and if the estate may exceed the federal exemption (~$24,000,000/person projected 2045), engage estate counsel (lifetime gifting at $19K/recipient/yr, portability, trust structure).

Sources: BOE Prop 19 rules · IRS Topic 409 — stepped-up basis / $0 gain on immediate sale · IRS estate tax page
