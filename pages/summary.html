Executive Summary

Decision context: two properties today; one inherited house (Property A, Cupertino) plus cash expected around 2040–2050; the SF house (Property B) is the current home and income engine.

The five things that matter most

  1. California has no estate or inheritance tax. For deaths on/after 2005-01-01 no CA estate tax return is even required; local death taxes don't exist in CA. VERIFIED SCO — California Estate Tax
  2. Federal estate tax starts at $15,000,000 per person for 2026 deaths (40% top rate; $30M per couple with portability). The cash portion of the inheritance is the only thing likely to touch this. VERIFIED IRS Estate Tax
  3. Proposition 19 is the biggest single money lever on Property A. If the heir does not move in (or the house wasn't the transferor's principal residence), the house is reassessed to full market value: modeled 2040 tax jumps from ~$9,700/yr (status quo) to ~$73,600/yr without the exclusion, or ~$59,700/yr even with it (FMV − $1,044,586 cap). VERIFIED law PROJECTION numbers BOE Proposition 19
  4. Inheriting resets the cost basis to date-of-death value (federal §1014), so a sale soon after inheritance owes tax only on post-death appreciation — at 0/15/20% federal + 3.8% NIIT + up to 13.3% CA on that slice. VERIFIED IRS Pub 551
  5. Property B's rental rules are stable and verified: SF allows 1.6% increases on occupied units for 2026-03-01→2027-02-28; Costa-Hawkins lets market rent be reset at turnover; AB 1482 backstops at 5%+CPI (max 10%) statewide. VERIFIED SF Rent Board
