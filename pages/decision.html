Live, rent, or sell?

Use the current-law decision gates first; only then compare cash flow and investment returns. There is no supported numeric winner from the information that can safely be verified today.

Privacy and evidence. Household-specific figures supplied for this analysis are not reproduced. Any financial conclusion based on those figures would be unverified until matched to official parcel records, insurance quotes, leases, and tax documents.

A streamlined choice sequence

  1. Confirm eligibility before optimizing. Establish who inherits, the transferor’s principal residence, the date of transfer/death, and which heir will actually occupy the home. Ask the county assessor to confirm the Prop. 19 rules and current exclusion cap.
  2. Build a property-level tax estimate. Obtain official enrolled assessed value, tax-rate area, secured bill, direct assessments, and any supplemental assessment. Apply the relevant law—not a ZIP-code average.
  3. Build three complete annual cash-flow cases. For each home option include rent actually collectible, vacancy, property tax, insurance quotes, water/electricity, repairs/capital reserves, management, debt, income tax, and sale tax where relevant.
  4. Compare after-tax outcomes. Include sale expenses and inherited basis, future taxes on rent/investment income, investment risk, liquidity needs, and value of living in each location. Use low/base/high scenarios only for clearly labeled assumptions.
  5. Set a re-check date. Re-run the analysis at inheritance and again after any law, family, title, insurance, or property-condition change.
Conditional recommendation: If a beneficiary would genuinely choose the inherited home as their primary residence even without a tax benefit, evaluate living there first because that is the route most relevant to the current Prop. 19 family-home exclusion. If the beneficiary would not live there, do not force a move solely from an unverified tax estimate; compare rent and sell on complete after-tax numbers. No recommendation to sell the San Francisco property is supportable without its verified net operating income and housing value.

The comparison is a framework, not individualized investment advice. Tax residence, co-ownership, rental classification, capital gains, and Prop. 19 eligibility can be fact-sensitive.
