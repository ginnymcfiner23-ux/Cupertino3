The decision in one paragraph

Recommendation: Sell the Cupertino house at inheritance and reinvest the proceeds in the stock market; keep the San Francisco house (either live in the third unit or keep renting all three units). Under Proposition 19 (in effect since Feb 16, 2021), an inherited rental property is reassessed to full market value — the Cupertino house's property tax would jump from ~$7,207 today to about $72,247 per year by 2045 (moderate growth scenario), which exceeds any plausible net rental income (projected net operating income is about −$53,787/yr). Because inherited assets receive a stepped-up basis at date of death, selling the house immediately after inheritance produces $0 capital gains tax, and the proceeds (~$5,602,169 net by 2045) invested at a 6% nominal return generate roughly $299,044/yr after tax — without property tax, insurance, maintenance, or vacancy risk. The SF house keeps its Prop 19 capped exclusion if the heir moves into it within one year (and files the homeowner's exemption), or its $7,150/mo rent covers a full reassessment.

Key findings

1. There is no California inheritance tax — the only estate tax is federal

California repealed its inheritance tax in 1982 and has no state estate tax (the old "pick-up" tax collects $0). The only inheritance tax exposure is the federal estate tax: $15,000,000 per person ($30M per married couple with portability) in 2026, made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) and indexed for inflation from 2027. Estate tax is 40% on the amount above the exemption and is paid by the estate (Form 706, due 9 months after death), not by heirs. Inheritances themselves are not income to recipients.

Sources: IRS estate tax page · IRS IR-2025-103 (2026 inflation adjustments) · California FTB — estate tax history

2. Proposition 19 is the single biggest cost driver at inheritance

Prop 19 (effective Feb 16, 2021) eliminated the parent-child property-tax exclusion for anything other than the family home. Inherited rental/investment property is reassessed to full market value; inherited principal residences get the factored base year value plus an inflation-adjusted $1,044,586 allowance (2025–2027 period) — but only if the heir occupies it as their primary residence within one year and files the homeowner's exemption. The claim form is BOE-19-P, due within 3 years of the transfer (date of death). A repeal initiative failed to qualify for the November 2026 ballot; Prop 19 remains in effect.

Sources: BOE Taxpayers' Rights Advocate (official Prop 19 guidance) · CA Secretary of State — repeal initiative failed

3. Total property tax at inheritance: ~$96K/yr by 2045 (both houses kept)

Today the two houses cost about $7,207 + $2,439 = $9,646/yr in property tax. Under Prop 19 at a 2045 inheritance (moderate 3.5% growth), keeping both houses costs roughly $96,256/yr (Cupertino fully reassessed; SF with the capped exclusion). The result is nearly identical if the parents instead make the Cupertino house their principal residence — the $1M-capped exclusion simply shifts between the two houses.

4. Selling is tax-free; renting is a net loss; living in Cupertino costs ~$80K/yr

Stepped-up basis means $0 capital gains tax if the inherited house is sold at market value (IRS Topic 409). Renting it out after the reassessment produces a projected net operating loss of about $53,787/yr by 2045. Living in it avoids rent but still carries ~$72,247 in property tax plus insurance, utilities and maintenance — roughly $79K/yr of housing cost. The SF house's rent ($7,150/mo = $85,800/yr) comfortably absorbs its own reassessment.

Sources: IRS Topic 409 — capital gains & stepped-up basis · IRS Pub 551 — basis of assets

5. Planning actions to take now (before 2040)

  1. Fund the trust with both houses (a trust only avoids probate for assets titled into it).
  2. Decide which house is the parents' principal residence — it determines which house gets the Prop 19 capped exclusion and which is fully reassessed.
  3. Verify all user-provided figures against official tax bills, assessor records, leases and quotes (see Flagged for Review).
  4. If the estate may exceed ~$30M (couple), plan with an estate attorney: lifetime gifting ($19,000/recipient annual exclusion in 2026), portability, and trust structure.
  5. Heirs must move into the excluded house within 1 year of inheritance and file the homeowner's exemption, or the exclusion is lost permanently.
