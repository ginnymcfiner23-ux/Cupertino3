F1HighIrregularityProperty B property tax is roughly double what San Francisco's official rate implies
The owner's figures show 2025 tax of 2,439 USD on a 2025 assessment of 114,248 USD, an implied effective rate of 2.13%. San Francisco's verified combined secured ad valorem rate for FY2025-26 is 1.18268325% (SF Board of Supervisors Resolution 447-25), which would produce roughly 1,351 USD on that assessment. The 2025-26 gap is about 1,088 USD. The gap is present in every year of the supplied series and ranges from 1.73% to 2.10%.
Possible explanations: The tax figure includes direct charges and special assessments that are not ad valorem.; The assessment figure is incomplete, for example land only, or a single unit's share.; The tax figure is an escrow or aggregator number that bundles something else.		Read the line items off the actual SF secured property tax bill and compare the ad valorem portion to 1.18268325% of the net taxable value. Then correct the assessment figure if it is incomplete.Every Property B holding-cost projection on this site inherits this error. Until it is resolved, treat Property B cost figures as unreliable.SF Board of Supervisors Resolution No. 447-25
F2MediumIrregularityProperty A implied effective rate is above the range typically quoted for Santa Clara County
The owner's figures show 2025 tax of 7,207 USD on a 2025 assessment of 539,743 USD, an implied effective rate of 1.335%. Secondary sources describe Santa Clara County totals as generally between about 1.12% and 1.24%. The implied rate drifts down over the supplied series, from about 1.41% in 2016 to about 1.34% in 2025, which is what you would expect as voter-approved bonds are paid off.
Possible explanations: The parcel is in a tax rate area with above-average voter-approved debt.; The bill includes direct assessments, for example a water district or vector control charge.; The assessment figure is gross rather than net of the Homeowners' Exemption, which would make the true rate higher still.		Pull the parcel's tax rate area from the Santa Clara County Assessor and read the rate off the Controller-Treasurer's annual Compilation of Tax Rates.Property A tax projections use the derived 1.33% rate. A one-point error here moves the 2045 tax projection by tens of thousands of dollars a year.Santa Clara County Assessor, Annual Report 2025

F4HighUnverifiedWhich Net Energy Metering rule governs Property A's solar system is unknown
The owner reports a net credit of 10 to 20 USD a month. Systems interconnected under the older NEM 2.0 rules earn near-retail export credits for 20 years from interconnection. Systems under the current Net Billing Tariff earn hourly avoided-cost values and, per secondary sources describing the CPUC decision, about 75% less. Whether the reported credit is sustainable into the 2040s depends entirely on which regime the system is in and, if NEM 2.0, on when the 20-year term expires.		Locate the PG&E interconnection agreement or the original installer paperwork and read the interconnection date and the NEM vintage.Electricity is a small line item, so the direct effect is modest. The indirect effect is large: if the system is on a legacy NEM term that expires mid-hold, the holding cost jumps.—

F6MediumUnverifiedProperty A's water provider has not been identified
Cupertino is served by three separate water systems: California Water Service in the northeast, San Jose Water Company in the southeast, and the city's own system in the west, which San Jose Water operates under a lease running to 2036. A ZIP code does not determine which one serves a given parcel.		Look the address up privately on the City's Water Service Provider GIS map, then pull that utility's current tariff.Water is a small line item. The risk is that the wrong tariff is used and the projection is built on the wrong rate structure.City of Cupertino, Water
F7MediumUnverifiedWhether Property B is rent-controlled depends on its certificate-of-occupancy date
San Francisco's verified allowable increase is 1.6% for 2026-03-01 through 2027-02-28, but that applies only to covered units. The coverage rule, commonly tied to a certificate of occupancy on or before 1979-06-13 with single-family homes and condos exempt under Costa-Hawkins, was not retrieved from the Rent Ordinance in this pass. If the building is not covered, the statewide AB 1482 cap governs instead, and that cap is 5% plus regional CPI up to 10%, materially higher.		Get the certificate-of-occupancy date from the SF Department of Building Inspection, then confirm coverage with the Rent Board.Determines whether Property B rental income grows at roughly 1.6% or roughly 8.8% a year. Over twenty years that is a very large difference.City and County of San Francisco, Rent Board
