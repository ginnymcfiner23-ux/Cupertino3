Inheritance process, step by step

The sequence for Property A passing to the next generation through a revocable living trust, with the deadline and official citation for each step. Written generically: no name, trust name, date of death or parcel identifier appears.

The decision gate. Is Property A the grantor's principal residence at the date of transfer, and will the heir move in within one year?

If yes: Partial exclusion available: new taxable value = factored base year value + (fair market value − (factored base year value + the indexed $1 million amount)).

If no: No exclusion. Full reassessment to fair market value as of the date of death. Property tax rises roughly in proportion to the gap between the old Prop 13 base and current market value.

BOE Letter To Assessors 2026/026

  1. Confirm the trust holds title to Property A and that the successor-trustee designation is current
    Before death · deadline: Well before death · Owner input
    The analysis on this site assumes Property A passes through a revocable living trust rather than through probate, as the owner stated.
    Source: none
    Not verifiable from a public source. Confirm with the estate planning attorney.
  2. Confirm whether Property A is the grantor's principal residence
    Before death · deadline: At the date of transfer · Verified
    Under Proposition 19 the exclusion is available only for a 'family home' — a dwelling eligible for the Homeowners' or Disabled Veterans' Exemption because of the transferor's ownership and occupation. If Property A is not the grantor's principal residence at the date of transfer, no exclusion is available at all and the property is reassessed to full market value.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, LTA 2026/026 (Definitions, item 7; Principal Residences Q1)
    This is the highest-leverage fact in the entire project. Proof of residency can include vehicle registration, voter registration, bank accounts, or income tax records.
  3. A change in ownership occurs on the date of death
    Date of death · deadline: Date of death · Verified
    For property held in a revocable trust, a change in ownership occurs when interests vest in persons other than the trustor or the trustor's spouse, which typically happens on the trustor's death. The date of death is treated as the date of change in ownership, and the law in effect on that date controls.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, Proposition 19 FAQ #9 (citing R&T Code 61(h) and Property Tax Rule 462.260)
    For a death in the 2040-2050 window, Proposition 19 will govern. Proposition 58/193 applies only to transfers on or before 2021-02-15.
  4. File a Death Statement with the County Assessor
    Within 150 days · deadline: 150 days · Verified
    Whenever there is a change in ownership caused by the death of an owner of real property, the transferee must file a Death Statement with the County Assessor in the county where the property is located. The Santa Clara County Assessor confirms this is required even when the property was held in the decedent's trust.
    Source: Office of the Assessor, County of Santa Clara, Annual Report 2025
    Applies to Property A. The form and any San Francisco equivalent should be confirmed with the SF Assessor-Recorder closer to the date.
  5. The Assessor enrolls a new assessed value
    Automatically, on reassessment · deadline: Set by the Assessor · Verified
    Absent a qualifying exclusion, the Assessor reassesses the property to its current fair market value as of the date of death. Only the portion that changed ownership is reassessed.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, Change in Ownership FAQ
    A supplemental assessment is issued for the difference between the old and new assessed values for the remainder of the fiscal year.
  6. Move into Property A and make it the heir's principal residence
    Within 1 year — only if claiming the exclusion · deadline: 1 year after transfer · Verified
    The transfer must be of the transferor's principal residence and the transferee must make it their own principal residence within one year after the transfer. The BOE states there are no exceptions to this rule.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, LTA 2026/026 (Occupancy Q7); R&T Code 63.2(a)(1)(A)
    Skipping this step means full reassessment to market value. Quantified on the Scenarios page.
  7. File the Homeowners' Exemption (BOE-266) or Disabled Veterans' Exemption (BOE-261-G) with the County Assessor
    Within 1 year — only if claiming the exclusion · deadline: 1 year from date of death or transfer · Verified
    Filing within one year of the date of death or transfer is a condition of the exclusion. If filed after the one-year window, the exclusion is applied prospectively only.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, Proposition 19 Filing Requirements Checklist
    Separate from and additional to the BOE-19-P claim in step 8. Both are required.
  8. File BOE-19-P, Claim for Reassessment Exclusion for Transfer Between Parent and Child
    Within 3 years — only if claiming the exclusion · deadline: 3 years from date of death or transfer, or before transfer to a third party, whichever is earlier · Verified
    File with the County Assessor of the county where the property is located.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, Proposition 19 Filing Requirements Checklist
    R&T Code 63.2(f)(1)(A). Missing this deadline forfeits the exclusion entirely.
  9. Keep Property A as the principal residence
    Ongoing, only if the exclusion was claimed · deadline: Continuous · Verified
    The exclusion requires continual use as a family home by an eligible transferee. If the home stops being eligible for the Homeowners' or Disabled Veterans' Exemption, the exclusion is removed and the new base year value is enrolled on the lien date following the loss of eligibility.
    Source: California State Board of Equalization, LTA 2026/026 (Continual Use Requirement; Occupancy Q8)
    This is the trap in the 'move in for a year, then rent it out' idea. Renting it out later removes the exclusion.
  10. Determine whether Form 706 is required
    Federal, within 9 months if required · deadline: 9 months after date of death · Verified
    A federal estate tax return is required if the gross estate, increased by adjusted taxable gifts and specific gift tax exemption, exceeds the filing threshold for the year of death — $15,000,000 for 2026, and indexed thereafter.
    Source: IRS, Estate tax
    Even an estate well below the threshold must file a timely return if the executor wants to elect portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion.
  11. Establish the income tax basis of the inherited assets
    Federal · deadline: At the date of death · Verified
    Property acquired from a decedent takes a basis equal to its fair market value at the date of death. This eliminates the unrealized gain that accrued during the decedent's lifetime.
    Source: IRS Publication 551, Basis of Assets (Rev. December 2025), 'Inherited Property'
    Document the date-of-death value. If the property is later sold, that documented value becomes the basis and is what stands between the estate and a large capital gain.
  12. Confirm no California death tax is due
    California · deadline: N/A · Verified
    R&T Code 13302 imposes a California estate tax only to the extent of the federal Credit for State Death Taxes. Congress repealed that credit, so the California tax computes to zero. No California estate tax return is due on that account.
    Source: California Legislative Information, R&T Code 13302
    Flagged: the federal repeal citation was not independently retrieved in this pass. The conclusion that no California estate tax is currently collected rests on the statutory text plus that repeal.
  13. Budget for transfer taxes and selling costs
    If the property is later sold · deadline: At closing · Unverified
    A recorded sale of San Francisco real property triggers the City's documentary transfer tax. Santa Clara County and its cities have their own transfer taxes. The rates were not retrieved from official sources in this pass.
    Source: none
    Left blank deliberately. Confirm the current rate with the SF Treasurer & Tax Collector and with Santa Clara County before modelling net sale proceeds. The scenarios on this site use an assumption instead, clearly labelled.
