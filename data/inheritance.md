# INHERITANCE PROCESS — STEP BY STEP (Cupertino & SF houses, via trust, target 2040-2050)

Based on verified law (see data/law-tax.md). Each step cites its source. Items needing legal review are flagged.

## Before death (now → 2040)

1. Create/confirm a revocable living trust (or irrevocable trust for estate-tax planning) and FUND it — deed both houses into the trust. A trust only avoids probate for assets actually titled into it.
   - Source: California Probate Code (trusts); summaries — https://californiawilltemplate.com/blog/how-to-avoid-probate-ca/ (flag: verify with attorney / leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)
2. Decide which house will be the principal residence of the transferors (parents). This determines which house qualifies for the Prop 19 parent-child exclusion at death (family home only). The other house (rental) will be fully reassessed at market value.
   - Source: BOE LTA 2022/051 — https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22051.pdf
3. If estate may exceed the federal exemption, consider lifetime gifting (annual exclusion $19,000/recipient in 2026) or other estate-tax planning. Confirm with an estate attorney.
   - Source: IRS IR-2025-103 — https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
4. Keep the trust funded with current title; review beneficiaries; maintain insurance, utilities, repairs.
5. Consider whether to sell/refinance before death — note: selling before death triggers capital gains tax on appreciation since purchase (no step-up for the seller); inheriting first gives heirs a stepped-up basis. Generally, selling AFTER inheritance is tax-advantaged.
   - Source: IRS Topic 409 — https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409

## At death (2040-2050)

6. Death occurs. Date of death = date of transfer for property tax purposes (not the distribution date).
   - Source: BOE LTA 2022/051 — https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22051.pdf
7. Successor trustee takes control of the trust assets (no probate court). If assets were NOT in a trust, heirs must open a probate case (public, statutory fees on gross estate) — or use small-estate/AB 2016 tools if eligible.
   - Source: Probate Code (flag: verify current statute text on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov)
8. County assessor reassesses both houses as of the date of death (change of ownership):
   - Cupertino (rental): fully reassessed to market value at date of death — NO Prop 19 exclusion.
   - SF (principal residence): Prop 19 parent-child exclusion available IF the child moves in within 1 year and files the homeowner's exemption; new taxable value = market value − ($1M inflation-adjusted allowance) when market value exceeds factored base year value + allowance.
   - Source: BOE LTA 2022/051 — https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22051.pdf
9. Heirs file Form BOE-19-P (claim for exclusion) with the county assessor within 3 years of the date of transfer (or before any sale to a third party). For the SF family home: file the homeowner's exemption within 1 year of transfer.
   - Source: BOE LTA 2022/051 — https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22051.pdf
10. Supplemental tax bills are issued for the partial fiscal year(s) following the reassessment; heirs pay the prorated amounts.
    - Source: SF Treasurer — https://sftreasurer.org/property/secured-property-taxes ; BOE LTA 2022/051
11. If the estate's gross value + taxable gifts exceeds the federal exemption for the year of death, the estate files Form 706 (due 9 months after death) and pays 40% estate tax on the excess; portability election if the surviving spouse is to receive the unused exclusion.
    - Source: IRS estate tax page — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estate-tax
12. Heirs receive stepped-up basis on inherited assets (FMV at date of death) → selling inherited real estate at market value soon after inheritance generally triggers $0 capital gains tax.
    - Source: IRS Pub 551 / Topic 409 — https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-publication-551 ; https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409

## After inheritance (decision point)

13. DECIDE what to do with the Cupertino house: live / rent / sell. (See docs/scenarios.html for the analysis and recommendation.)
14. If keeping the SF family home: the child must occupy it as their primary residence and file the homeowner's exemption — otherwise the exclusion is lost and the house is fully reassessed. If the child later moves out (making it a rental), the exclusion is removed and a new value is established.
    - Source: BOE LTA 2022/051 — https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/pdf/lta22051.pdf
15. Ongoing: pay property taxes (now at reassessed values), insurance, utilities, maintenance; file income tax on rental income; track capital gains basis from date of death for future sales.

## Key deadlines checklist (Cupertino/SF at inheritance)
- [ ] Confirm trust is funded with both houses (before death)
- [ ] At death: order death certificate; successor trustee takes over
- [ ] Within 3 years: file BOE-19-P claim (SF family home exclusion; Cupertino has no exclusion)
- [ ] Within 1 year: file homeowner's exemption on SF family home; child must occupy as primary residence
- [ ] Pay supplemental property tax bills issued after reassessment
- [ ] Within 9 months: file Form 706 if estate exceeds exemption (or elect portability)
- [ ] If selling inherited property: basis = FMV at date of death (obtain appraisal); sell at/above basis → no capital gains tax
