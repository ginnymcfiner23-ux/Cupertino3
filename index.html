Executive summary

Most important tax fork

Prop. 19 primary-residence eligibility

Under current California rules, an eligible parent-to-child home transfer may preserve part of the Prop. 13 assessed-value base only if the statutory family-home conditions are met, including qualifying principal-residence use. The rules and indexed value limit must be checked at the future date of death.

Current federal baseline

Estate threshold is about the whole taxable estate

For 2026 deaths, the federal basic exclusion is $15 million per decedent, plus any available deceased-spousal unused exclusion. It is not a tax-free amount per house or per beneficiary; adjusted taxable lifetime gifts and the gross estate matter.

Provisional decision rule: Do not commit to rent or sell before checking whether a genuine move-in as the heir’s principal residence would preserve a valuable Prop. 19 exclusion. Compare that property-tax effect with the net rent forgone, housing costs avoided, and the after-tax risk-adjusted return on net sale proceeds. Keep the existing San Francisco property in the comparison as the alternative housing/income source—but do not assume its apparent rent is net profit.
