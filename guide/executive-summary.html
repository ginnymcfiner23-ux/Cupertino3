Executive summary

This guide compares three options for two inherited properties: keep and live in them (Prop 19 family-home treatment, where it applies), keep and rent them, or sell and invest the proceeds. It is built from official rules, client inputs, and a year-by-year model to 2050.

Key findings (base case, inheritance in 2045, appreciation 2.15%/yr)

  • Property tax is the biggest change at inheritance. Property A's Prop 13 base is about $802,028. If the heir lives in it (Prop 19 family-home rule), the taxable value is about $3,599,398, giving an annual tax of about $42,473. If it is reassessed to market value, the annual tax would be about $54,799 (property-tax.html).
  • Property B's rented units would be reassessed. Prop 19 applies per unit (BOE LTA 2026/026). Only an owner-occupied unit can use the family-home treatment. Tax on B if fully reassessed is about $31,863 a year (rental-sf.html).
  • Federal estate tax is not expected from these two properties alone. The 2026 basic exclusion is $15,000,000 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Combined projected value at 2045 is about $7,340,491. California has no estate or inheritance tax (RTC 13301).
  • Heirs get a basis step-up to fair market value at death (IRC 1014). Gains are measured only from the date-of-death value.
  • Financial lean (not a recommendation). Excluding use value and income tax, selling and investing beats holding Property A through 2050 in the base case. For Property B, keeping it as rental and selling are close in the base case, and the answer depends on income tax and rent growth (see decision-guide.html).

Caveats

  • Client values (market values, assessed values, water, electricity, rent, insurance) are UNVERIFIED inputs. They are labelled throughout and are not verified facts.
  • Parcel-level tax rates were not confirmed. The Cupertino TRA composite is blank. The client's tax history implies rates above official sampled totals (see Flagged).
  • Prop 19 requires that the decedent's home was their principal residence and that the heir moves in within one year. If not, the reassessed figures apply.
  • This is informational analysis, not legal or tax advice. Confirm with an estate attorney and CPA before acting.
