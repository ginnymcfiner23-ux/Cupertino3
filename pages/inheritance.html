Inheritance steps

Category • Trust and inheritance process

What the transfer process generally looks like

Use this as a sequence for coordination with the successor trustee, county assessor, CPA, and estate attorney. Trust documents and the decedent’s facts control; this is not a promise that probate or any particular filing will be avoided.

StepActionTiming / control pointPrimary-source link
1. Secure authority and recordsSuccessor trustee confirms authority under the trust, obtains death certificates, secures property, reviews deeds, insurance, mortgage, leases, and account ownership; use secure channels for identity documents.Promptly after death; follow the trust and professional advice. Exact notice duties depend on trust and California law.California Courts: wills, estates, and advance care planning
2. Inventory and value the taxable estateList real estate, cash, investments, business interests, insurance, debts, lifetime taxable gifts, and ownership interests; document date-of-death values and any alternate-valuation election question.At date of death; threshold is applied to gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts, not just the inherited home or each beneficiary’s share.IRS estate tax FAQ
3. Evaluate Form 706 and portabilityExecutor/CPA checks Form 706 filing requirement, estate tax, generation-skipping transfer considerations, and whether a timely portability election is appropriate for a surviving spouse.Generally due within 9 months after death; an extension may be requested. Confirm the then-current rules and deadlines.IRS Instructions for Form 706
4. Secure basis and income recordsKeep appraisal and basis records for inherited assets. Track post-death rent, interest, dividends, and other income separately; inherited principal and income produced by inherited assets are treated differently.Before distributing or selling assets; obtain advice on rental depreciation and later sale.IRS estate FAQ: inherited property basis
5. Report ongoing estate/trust incomeDetermine federal and California fiduciary income tax returns, allocation of income, deductions, and beneficiary statements, including California Form 541 where required.Based on estate/trust tax year and filing status; use current-year forms, not old deadlines.California FTB estates and trusts filing requirements
6. Report change of ownership to countyFile required change-in-ownership information and obtain the actual supplemental/annual assessment. A transfer through a trust is not by itself a guarantee of a Prop. 19 exclusion.Follow the county’s current forms, deadlines, and requests; keep all notices and proof of filing.Santa Clara County Assessor: Prop. 19
7. Decide primary residence and claimIf pursuing the current intergenerational family-home exclusion, verify relationship and family-home conditions; an eligible transferee must establish the home as their principal residence and apply for the applicable exemption within the required period.Current assessor guidance states one year for move-in/exemption; claim timing also applies. Reconfirm rules and indexed amount at transfer.BOE Prop. 19 claim forms · Santa Clara County Assessor guidance
8. Settle liabilities and make distributionsPay valid trust/estate expenses and taxes, maintain reserves, record distributions, and coordinate deeds/accounts with the trustee and title professionals.Trust terms and applicable law govern; do not distribute assets before confirming obligations.Santa Clara County Superior Court probate guidance
9. Obtain insurance and operate safelyNotify insurer of ownership/occupancy change; obtain the correct owner-occupied or rental policy, replacement-cost basis, liability limits, and any separate earthquake/flood coverage if needed.Before occupancy or tenant changes; policy terms and eligibility are insurer-specific.California Department of Insurance residential guide
Escalation flag: A large cash/investment inheritance can change the federal estate-tax filing analysis even if neither home alone is near the threshold. The threshold is applied at the decedent/estate level and reduced by prior taxable gifts and affected by deductions and spouse portability; request a confidential estate inventory and professional tax estimate.

No personal information appears here. Do not put trust documents, parcel numbers, taxpayer IDs, beneficiary identities, appraisals, leases, or financial account statements on this public website.

Educational decision support only; not legal, tax, insurance, investment, or appraisal advice. Law, rates, eligibility, and forms can change before a future transfer. Household-specific information is intentionally excluded. Review primary sources.
