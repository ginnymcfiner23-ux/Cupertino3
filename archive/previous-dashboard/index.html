02 / DECISION FRAMEWORK

Keep, rent, or sell?

There is no defensible “best” choice without verified inheritance-date tax basis, title/trust terms, net rent, actual insurance quotes, maintenance history, and household goals. Compare after-tax, risk-adjusted cash flows at the time—not gross appreciation alone.

A

Live in the inherited home

Test if expected housing value to household exceeds incremental property tax, insurance, utilities, upkeep, commute and lost alternative rental income. Evaluate Prop 19 eligibility within one year.

Likely fit: housing need + eligibility + sustainable carrying cost.
B

Rent the home

Model collected rent less vacancy, management, repairs, capital reserves, insurance, tax, utilities paid by owner, and income tax. Rental use generally does not meet the continued principal-residence condition for Prop 19.

Likely fit: durable positive net income and willingness to manage landlord obligations.
C

Sell and reinvest

Estimate net proceeds after selling costs and any taxable gain using properly documented inherited basis; compare diversified investment alternatives, taxes, volatility, and liquidity.

Likely fit: low property-specific risk concentration or capital needed elsewhere.
Connection to the other residence: assess the existing multi-unit rental/owner-occupied property as a separate household asset. Its rent, vacancy, costs, financing, unit legality, and insurance affect liquidity and risk capacity, but no private property figures are reproduced here.

03 / TRANSFER ROADMAP

How the inheritance process may unfold

  1. Locate controlling documents. Trustee/executor confirms trust, will, title vesting, beneficiaries, powers, debts and distribution terms. Obtain advice before transferring or retitling.
  2. Establish authority and inventory. Fiduciary identifies real estate, cash/securities, liabilities, insurance, and any tax filing obligations; preserve records and secure the property.
  3. Value assets as of the relevant date. Obtain qualified date-of-death valuations and appraisals. IRS states inherited-property basis is generally fair market value at death, subject to exceptions and elections.
  4. Assess estate tax and filings. Aggregate the decedent’s includible assets, adjusted taxable gifts and relevant deductions; consider federal filing and portability even if no tax is payable.
  5. Handle title and county claims. Notify assessor/recorder as required; evaluate Prop 19 eligibility, deadlines, and multi-unit allocation with the county. Do not assume trust ownership itself avoids reassessment.
  6. Set operating plan. Obtain binding insurance quotes, inspections, utility bills, repair scope, rental compliance review, and tax projections for each choice.
  7. Decide, document, revisit. Compare net outcomes with a CPA, estate attorney, fiduciary, insurance broker, and financial adviser. Review laws and market inputs immediately before distribution/action.

06 / FLAGGED FOR REVIEW

Unverified items & irregularities

No estimate silently promoted to fact
  • User-provided property values, tax histories, rents, utilities, solar credits, insurance guesses and property descriptions were not published to protect privacy; none were independently verified.
  • No specific parcel identifiers or legal unit status were provided for public verification; exact property tax histories and assessed values therefore remain blank.
  • 2026 is a current-law snapshot only. A 2040–2050 house-price, tax, inflation, insurance, rent or investment-return projection would require assumptions and would not be an official verified value; intentionally not fabricated.
  • Insurance price cannot be established from ZIP-level research alone. Use the California DOI comparison tool and obtain property-specific quotes. FAIR Plan coverage is limited and may need complementary coverage.
  • Prop 19 depends on relationship, transferor’s prior residence, recipient occupancy, value cap, filing deadlines, and property configuration. Trust and multi-unit facts require individualized review.
  • 2026 federal $15 million estate filing threshold is not the same as the value a beneficiary can receive tax-free in all circumstances. Estate composition, lifetime gifts, spouse portability, deductions and applicable law matter.
  • California inheritance-tax status and estate-specific state exposure require confirmation by qualified counsel; no unsupported categorical answer is presented.
