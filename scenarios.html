Scenarios & recommendation

Three options for Property C at the moment of transfer, modelled over twenty years. The model assumes the property is inherited free of mortgage debt, that the transfer happens in 2045, and that no other property changes hands.

Recommended: Sell and reinvest. Projected net position twenty years after transfer: $17,335,884, versus $10,052,372 for Rent Property C and $7,168,260 for Live in Property C.

Two things this scenario locks in. Moving in commits the heir to continual owner-occupancy: if they later rent the house out, the exclusion is removed and the new base year value is enrolled. And it spends the stepped-up basis window, which is the one moment at which the home can be sold with almost no taxable gain. Instead of an asset compounding in a portfolio, the heir holds one costing over $105,239 a year to carry.

Depreciation is modelled at 80% of value over 27.5 years, both assumptions. Accumulated depreciation of $3,989,989 reduces the basis on a later sale to $2,541,243, which is why the liquidation tax is higher here. Depreciation recapture at a different rate than the capital gains rate is not modelled — the F8 rate gap means this is approximate.

At the assumed rent this scenario loses cash from day one. Carrying cost of $122,342 exceeds gross rent of $102,600. The break-even rent is about $10,732 a month. Because market rent for Property C is a placeholder, see the sensitivity below before drawing a conclusion.

What would change the answer

A much higher rent for Property C

The sell case is strongest while carrying cost exceeds rent. If Property C could reliably rent for well above $10,732 a month, the rent scenario improves markedly. That is an empirical question, and it is currently unanswered. See F3.

A change in Proposition 19

Three repeal attempts have now failed, the most recent confirmed by the Secretary of State on 2026-05-18. If a future measure restored the old Prop 58/193 rules, the reassessment would disappear for a principal residence and the holding cost would fall dramatically, which would reverse the recommendation. Track it. Pending Law Changes →

Non-financial reasons to keep the house

This model prices money, not meaning. Living in a family home has value that is not in the table. What the model does say is what that choice costs: roughly $10,167,624 of projected net position over twenty years, or about $508,381 a year.

A mortgage or a co-heir

The model assumes the property is inherited free of debt and by a single heir. Debt service, or a buyout of siblings, changes the cash-flow picture substantially and could force a sale regardless of preference.
