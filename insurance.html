Why the market data still matters

Premiums are under pressure statewide

California homeowners direct written premium rose from $12.1bn in 2022 to $13.2bn in 2023, and the loss ratio rose from 55.17% to 63.34%. A rising loss ratio is a leading indicator of continued rate pressure. California Department of Insurance

Premium tracks replacement cost, not market value

Both properties sit in markets where land drives most of the value. Insurance prices the cost to rebuild the structure, not the sale price. That is why a $3.1m Cupertino house may insure for far less than a naive percentage-of-value rule would suggest. This is an observation about how policies are priced, not a verified premium.

Two gaps in the model

Earthquake cover is not priced

Earthquake damage is excluded from standard California homeowners policies and is normally bought separately. No rate was retrieved. Both properties are in a high-seismic-risk region, so the true holding cost is understated until this is priced or an explicit decision is made to self-insure. See F19.

FAIR Plan as insurer of last resort

If admitted carriers decline to quote, the California FAIR Plan is the fallback. ZIP-level FAIR Plan premium data is reported by news organisations from FAIR Plan disclosures, but was not confirmed from an official FAIR Plan source in this pass. See F5.
