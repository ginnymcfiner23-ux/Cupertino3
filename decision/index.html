Recommendation

Sell both properties promptly after inheritance, unless the checks below change the answer

  1. Wealth comes out ahead in every case tested. In all three inheritance years and all three stress cases, selling and investing the net proceeds beat living in Property A and keeping either property in Property B (table above). This holds at the base-case end date of 2060.
  2. Property A has little net yield once carrying costs are counted. In 2045 (base), cash costs are about $73,532 per year, while the after-tax return forgone on about $4,702,573 of sale proceeds is about $255,726. Expected appreciation offsets about $123,895, leaving a net annual economic cost of about $205,363. Living value is not counted; compare it with a market rent for a comparable home.
  3. Property A rent only works above break-even. To rent instead of sell, rent must reach about $12,544 per month in 2026 dollars (base case, 2045; about $20,054 per month in 2045 dollars). Verified market rent data were not available, so this is a threshold to test, not a result.
  4. Property B keeps the rent but loses to selling. Keeping the two rented units and having the heir live in one (Prop 19) is worth about $175,583 in real terms compared with no occupancy. Even the best case for keeping (1% levy only, 0.5% upkeep, rent growing with inflation) reaches about $2,506,450, against $2,609,711 for selling.
  5. Sale right after inheritance should carry little capital-gain tax. Inherited property generally takes a basis equal to its value at death (IRC 1014). Confirm the rule with Publication 551 (see FL-07).
  6. Selling reduces exposure to law changes and to unverified inputs. It removes annual rent-cap and Prop 19 indexing risk, the unresolved SF tax-rate question (FL-03), the unverified solar and insurance figures, and the risk of a future change to Prop 19 (the 2026 repeal initiative failed, but rules can change again).

Checks that would change the answer (verify before acting)

  • Property A: if an heir wants to live there, complete the Prop 19 steps (occupy within one year; BOE-266; BOE-19-P) and compare the net annual cost with comparable market rent (FL-01).
  • Property B: keep only if the SF bill shows the 2.13% rate is mostly a 1% levy, upkeep is well below 1%, rents track the cap, and the occupied unit is confirmed as qualifying (FL-03, FL-16, FL-17).
  • Replace client figures with appraisals, 12 months of bills, and written insurance quotes, then re-run scripts/model.py.
