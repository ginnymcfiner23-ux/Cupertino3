Methodology

How this site was built, what counts as verified, and every assumption feeding the projections.

The verification rule

A value is published with a Verified badge only if it was read directly from an official government or agency document during this pass, and the row links to that document. If no official source could be found, the value is left blank and marked Unverified. Values are never inferred, interpolated, or filled in from a secondary source.

Secondary sources — law-firm blogs, aggregator sites, news coverage — were consulted to find primary documents, but never to populate a value. Where a secondary source pointed to a figure that could not be confirmed, the row is blank and the item is on Flagged for Review.

Model structure

Projections

  1. Property value grows from the owner's November 2026 estimate at the assumed appreciation rate.
  2. Factored base year value grows at the verified 2% Prop 13 cap.
  3. At transfer, the new taxable value is computed with the R&T Code 63.2 formula: base year value plus the excess of market value over (base year value + indexed amount) if the heir moves in, or full market value if not.
  4. After transfer the new taxable value grows at the 2% cap.
  5. Annual property tax is taxable value times the effective rate.
  6. Insurance, water and electricity inflate at the assumed general inflation rate. Maintenance is a percentage of market value.
  7. Free cash flow each year is reinvested at the net portfolio growth rate.
  8. Net position at year 20 is asset value, plus reinvested cash flows, less tax on a hypothetical liquidation.

Known limitations

No mortgage, no co-heirs, no probate

The model assumes the property is inherited free of debt by a single heir, through a trust rather than probate. Debt service or a sibling buyout would change the cash-flow picture materially.

Capital gains rates are assumed

The combined federal and California capital gains rate used at liquidation is an assumption. Neither rate was confirmed from an official source. See F8.

Depreciation recapture is simplified

Scenario 2 applies a single capital gains rate to the whole gain, including the portion attributable to depreciation, which is normally recaptured at a different federal rate. The figures are approximate.

Section 121 is not modelled

An owner-occupant who lives in the home for two of the five years before sale can exclude $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for a joint return). That rule was not verified from an official source in this pass and is therefore not applied, which makes the "live in" scenario slightly pessimistic.

Reproducing this site

The site is generated from data/*.json by tools/build.js, using the projection engine in tools/model.js. Run node tools/build.js from the repository root. There are no dependencies and no network access is required to build. Every generated page is committed so GitHub Pages can serve it without a build step.
